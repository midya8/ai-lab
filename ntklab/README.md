# ntklab

The **Neural Tangent Kernel** from scratch in NumPy: a ReLU MLP with a hand-written per-sample backward pass, its *empirical* NTK, the closed-form *infinite-width* NTK/NNGP recursions, and the exact kernel-regression solution of gradient descent. Then six studies that measure when "a wide network is just kernel regression" is true, and when it stops being true. 38 tests; every number below is regenerated by `make_results.py` (about 8 minutes on 2 CPU cores, results in `results/*.json`).

Everything is synthetic and tiny (inputs on a circle, 1-D target), so read the numbers as a controlled lab, not as a claim about large models.

## What is in it

| Piece | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `MLP` | `net.py` | ReLU MLP, scalar output, NTK parametrisation (`W ~ N(0,1)`, widths scaling in the forward pass, He constants). Hand-written backprop; explicit `jacobian()` and a Jacobian-free `ntk()` (sum over layers of `(g g') * (a a')`) |
| `ntk`, `nngp`, `kernels` | `analytic.py` | infinite-width ReLU kernels via the arc-cosine recursion, any depth, with bias |
| `gd_predict` | `regression.py` | *exact* function-space result of `t` full-batch GD steps on the linearised model (eigendecomposition, stable as eigenvalues go to 0), plus `kernel_mean_prediction`, `stable_lr`, `residual_modes` |
| `train_gd` | `train.py` | real GD on the real network; optional output scale `alpha` with centring at init and step `lr/alpha^2` (the lazy-training knob of Chizat & Bach) |
| studies + CLI | `studies.py`, `__main__.py` | `python -m ntklab {convergence,lazy,ensemble,spectral,alpha,depth}` |

## Correctness checks (tests)

- Jacobian against central finite differences for depth 0-3 (with bias), loss gradient likewise, **and a deliberately perturbed Jacobian is shown to fail the same check**.
- `MLP.ntk` equals `J J^T` and the cross-kernel `J_A J_B^T` to 1e-9.
- Analytic kernel: symmetric PSD, linear-net closed form, a hand-computed one-hidden-layer value, preserved diagonal; the empirical NTK converges to it with width for depth 1-3, the mean over seeds is unbiased, the NNGP matches the covariance of 1500 random networks, and a wrong constant (kernel scaled by 0.5) is shown to be rejected.
- `gd_predict` equals explicit parameter-space GD on the linearised model to 1e-9, tends to interpolation and to the kernel-mean prediction, and `stable_lr` is shown to be stable while 1.2x it diverges.
- Training: deterministic, input net untouched, wide-net training tracks its linearisation, `alpha` makes training lazier.

```python
import numpy as np
from ntklab import MLP, ntk, gd_predict, stable_lr, train_gd

phi = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, 16, endpoint=False)
X = np.stack([np.cos(phi), np.sin(phi)], 1)
y = np.sin(phi) + 0.5 * np.sin(3 * phi)

net = MLP(in_dim=2, width=1024, depth=2, seed=0)
K = net.ntk(X)
lr = stable_lr(K, 0.5)
trained = train_gd(net, X, y, lr, steps=400)                  # real GD
_, lin = gd_predict(K, K, net(X), net(X), y, lr, steps=400)   # kernel prediction, same init
print(np.abs(trained.predict(X) - lin).max())                 # tiny: the net is (nearly) linear
print(np.linalg.norm(net.ntk(X) - ntk(X, depth=2)) / np.linalg.norm(ntk(X, depth=2)))   # ~0.1 at width 1024
```

## Results

Depth-2 nets, 16-24 points on a circle, target `sin(phi) + 0.5 sin(3 phi + 0.4)`; means over seeds.

### 1. The empirical kernel converges to the analytic one (`convergence`)

Relative Frobenius error of `Theta_hat` against `Theta_inf` (8 seeds, 24 points):

| width | 16 | 64 | 256 | 1024 | 4096 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean rel. error | 0.457 | 0.319 | 0.170 | 0.076 | 0.049 |

Log-log slope **-0.43** (theory: -0.5). The last point flattens a little - seed noise at 8 seeds, not a different regime (std 0.02).

### 2. Wide networks are lazy; narrow ones are not (`lazy`)

Train real nets with GD (600 steps, step = half the stability limit of each net's own kernel) and compare with the linearisation at the same initialisation (6 seeds):

| width | 8 | 32 | 128 | 512 | 2048 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| relative kernel movement (Frobenius, trained vs initial) | 0.83 | 0.33 | 0.077 | 0.033 | 0.011 |
| test-function gap net vs linearised | 0.31 | 0.18 | 0.080 | 0.042 | 0.018 |

Kernel movement falls with slope **-0.79** in width (faster than the 1/sqrt(width) bound that is usually quoted), the function gap with slope **-0.51**. At width 8 the "kernel" changes by 83 percent, so the NTK picture says nothing there.

### 3. Ensemble mean = NTK-GP mean (`ensemble`)

24 independently initialised width-512 networks trained 300 steps from `f_0` random. The ensemble-mean prediction is within **2.5%** of the analytic zero-mean NTK regression with the same step count; a single network is 5.5% off. The Monte-Carlo noise floor for a 24-member mean is about 1.1% (std-error estimate), so most of the remaining 2.5% is finite-width bias rather than sampling.

### 4. Spectral bias, measured (`spectral`)

Targets `sin(k phi)` on 96 points, width 2048, step = half the stability limit. Fraction of the target still unfit (`||f_t - y|| / ||y||`) after `t` steps, real net / linearised at the same init / analytic NTK. `lambda` is the kernel's Rayleigh quotient at that frequency.

| k | lambda | t=10 | t=50 | t=200 | t=1000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 32.4 | 0.010 / 0.011 / 0.004 | 0.005 / 0.005 / 0.000 | 0.003 / 0.004 / 0.000 | 0.002 / 0.002 / 0.000 |
| 2 | 10.3 | 0.234 / 0.232 / 0.238 | 0.013 / 0.014 / 0.001 | 0.008 / 0.010 / 0.000 | 0.004 / 0.006 / 0.000 |
| 4 | 1.32 | 0.848 / 0.845 / 0.842 | 0.462 / 0.433 / 0.423 | 0.067 / 0.051 / 0.032 | 0.009 / 0.019 / 0.000 |
| 8 | 0.263 | 0.968 / 0.967 / 0.966 | 0.862 / 0.847 / 0.843 | 0.643 / 0.519 / 0.505 | 0.299 / 0.067 / 0.033 |
| 16 | 0.067 | 0.992 / 0.991 / 0.991 | 0.965 / 0.958 / 0.958 | 0.908 / 0.843 / 0.841 | 0.828 / 0.432 / 0.422 |

- Low frequencies are fit orders of magnitude faster, and the ordering follows the kernel eigenvalue exactly.
- Early on (t <= 50) the real net, its own linearisation and the analytic kernel agree to the digit.
- **Two honest discrepancies.** (a) The floor of ~0.002-0.005 at k=1,2 is in the *linearised* column too: the finite-width kernel is not exactly circulant, so the mode is not an exact eigenvector. The analytic kernel is, hence its 1e-10. (b) For the slow modes (k=8, 16) late in training the *real* net is clearly slower than its linearisation (0.30 vs 0.07 at k=8, t=1000): the kernel moves (~1% overall, but its small eigendirections are the ones that matter for hard modes), so the lazy approximation is least accurate exactly where the learning is slowest.

### 5. Lazy to rich: scaling the output (`alpha`)

Width 256, output scaled by `alpha` and centred at init, step `lr/alpha^2` (same function-space dynamics). Median kernel movement and mean losses over 6 seeds:

| alpha | 1 | 3 | 10 | 30 | 100 | 300 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| parameter movement | 2.48 | 0.82 | 0.24 | 0.081 | 0.024 | 0.008 |
| kernel movement | 0.041 | 0.020 | 0.011 | 0.0043 | 0.0020 | 0.00008 |
| train loss | 4.7e-6 | 6.7e-6 | 4.0e-6 | 2.3e-6 | 2.3e-6 | 2.4e-6 |
| test loss | 0.00204 | 0.00263 | 0.00297 | 0.00305 | 0.00310 | 0.00313 |

Parameters move exactly like 1/alpha. The kernel moves 500x less across the range, but not smoothly: it plateaus around alpha 10-100 and only then drops. My reading (not separately verified) is that a few ReLU units flipping sign cause that plateau, and the clean 1/alpha rate appears once the displacement is too small to flip any. Every setting fits the training data equally well; **test loss is slightly *better* in the feature-learning regime (0.0020 vs 0.0031)**: a small but monotone effect here, on a task where the target is a smooth low-frequency function and 16 points are enough - I would not generalise it.

### 6. Depth changes the kernel, not just the capacity (`depth`)

Analytic NTK on 64 circle points:

| hidden layers | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| top eigenvalue / n | 0.45 | 0.80 | 1.18 | 1.58 | 2.46 | 3.44 |
| mean off-diagonal correlation | 0.37 | 0.43 | 0.45 | 0.47 | 0.49 | 0.50 |
| condition number (lambda_1/lambda_16) | 475 | 286 | 212 | 172 | 130 | 109 |

Deeper ReLU NTKs correlate all inputs more, grow their top eigenvalue linearly (so the stable step shrinks), and flatten the spectrum - which is why deep nets in the kernel regime fit high frequencies relatively faster, at the price of a smaller usable learning rate.

### 7. Closed-form LOO picks the ridge (`python -m ntklab.ridge`)

`ntklab/ridge.py` does kernel ridge with leave-one-out error from the hat matrix (no refits; one eigendecomposition covers the whole lambda grid; checked against explicit refits). Noisy circle data (noise 0.3), analytic depth-2 NTK, 20 seeds, test MSE:

| n | interpolation | LOO-chosen ridge | oracle ridge | median chosen lambda |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 0.089 | 0.070 | 0.064 | 0.10 |
| 40 | 0.062 | 0.033 | 0.031 | 0.18 |
| 80 | 0.057 | 0.020 | 0.017 | 0.32 |

Interpolating noisy data costs 2-3x test error at larger n; LOO lands within ~20% of the oracle without seeing test data. The chosen lambda grows with n here (the NTK sum scales with n). Synthetic, 20 seeds, no confidence intervals.

## Limits

- Scalar output, full-batch GD on MSE, ReLU only, one input geometry; no CNN/attention kernels, no finite-width corrections beyond what is measured empirically.
- Studies use 6-24 seeds on 16-96 points: they show trends and slopes, not tight confidence intervals.
- Everything is synthetic.
