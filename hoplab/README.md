# hoplab

Associative memory from scratch in NumPy: **classical Hopfield networks** (Hebbian, pseudoinverse and Storkey storage) next to **modern Hopfield networks** (dense associative memories with polynomial / exponential interactions, and the continuous-state version whose update *is* softmax attention). Everything is checked by tests (34) and the studies below are regenerated by `make_results.py`.

The point of the project is to *measure* why modern Hopfield networks matter: how many patterns each rule can hold, how much corruption it tolerates, what correlated patterns do to it, and where the inverse temperature of an attention head flips between "average everything" and "retrieve one memory".

## What is in it

| Piece | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Hebbian, pseudoinverse (projection), Storkey rules | `classical.py` | symmetric zero-diagonal weights; Storkey is tested against a naive triple-loop reference; projection W is verified idempotent with every pattern an eigenvector |
| Asynchronous dynamics + energy | `classical.py` | batched, strictly sequential per-state updates on the field from the *other* neurons, so E = -1/2 s^T W s never increases (tested per neuron update, and a deliberately wrong update rule is shown to raise it) |
| Dense associative memory (binary) | `modern.py` | E = -sum F(xi.s / N), rectified polynomial `x^n` or exponential `exp(beta x)`; asynchronous sign updates verified against brute-force lowest-energy choice; energy monotone per sweep |
| Continuous modern Hopfield | `modern.py` | `xi <- X^T softmax(beta X xi)`, the Ramsauer et al. energy (monotone, tested), `attention()` and a test that the update equals scaled dot-product attention with `beta = 1/sqrt(d)` |
| Diagnostics | `modern.py` | `effective_number` (exp-entropy of the attention weights: 1 = retrieval, p = global average), `separation` (distance to nearest rival pattern) |
| Patterns | `patterns.py`, `glyphs.py` | random and template-correlated +-1 patterns, flip/occlusion noise, ten 5x7 digit bitmaps |
| Studies + CLI | `studies.py`, `__main__.py` | `python -m hoplab capacity | stress | basin | correlated | beta | glyphs [--json]` |

## Quick start

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
PYTHONPATH=src python -m pytest -q           # 34 tests, ~1 s
PYTHONPATH=src python -m hoplab glyphs
PYTHONPATH=src python -m hoplab capacity
python make_results.py                       # rewrites results/ (about 45 s)
```

```python
import numpy as np
from hoplab import random_patterns, flip_noise, hebbian, retrieve, dam_retrieve

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
X = random_patterns(400, 100, rng)                  # 400 memories in 100 neurons (alpha = 4)
q = flip_noise(X[:5], 0.15, rng)                    # 15 percent of bits flipped
out = dam_retrieve(X, q, kind="exp", beta=12.0, rng=rng).states
print((out == X[:5]).all(axis=1))                   # all True; Hebbian cannot hold even 20 of these
```

## Results

All numbers are exact-recovery rates (every bit right) averaged over 3 seeds, N = 100 neurons unless stated. Raw tables: `results/`. These are synthetic patterns; "exact recovery from a noisy cue" is a strict criterion.

### 1. Capacity: load alpha = p / N, queries have 10 percent of bits flipped

| p | alpha | hebbian | pseudoinverse | storkey | dam-poly3 | dam-poly5 | dam-exp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.05 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 10 | 0.10 | 0.900 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 14 | 0.14 | 0.690 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 20 | 0.20 | 0.217 | 1.000 | 0.983 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 30 | 0.30 | 0.000 | 1.000 | 0.944 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 50 | 0.50 | 0.000 | 0.467 | 0.167 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 100 | 1.00 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 400 | 4.00 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |

Hebbian falls off a cliff around alpha ~ 0.14, consistent with the textbook 0.138 N limit (this strict criterion with noisy cues degrades a little before it). The pairwise rules all die by alpha ~ 0.5 to 1, because the capacity of pairwise networks scales only linearly in N. The higher-order dense memories do not.

Stress test, same setup but 25 percent flipped bits and loads up to 40 N (dam rules only):

| p | alpha | dam-poly3 | dam-poly5 | dam-exp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 1 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 400 | 4 | 0.833 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 1000 | 10 | 0.000 | 0.983 | 1.000 |
| 2000 | 20 | 0.000 | 0.983 | 0.983 |
| 4000 | 40 | 0.000 | 0.883 | 0.933 |

Cubic interactions hold out to alpha ~ 4 here, quintic and exponential keep going well past it (the exponential in N capacity of Demircigil et al. is a theory result; this run only shows 40 N, with `beta = 12` fixed and untuned).

### 2. Basin of attraction: alpha = 0.2 (p = 20), vary the corruption

| flipped bits | hebbian | pseudoinverse | storkey | dam-poly3 | dam-exp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.533 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 10% | 0.317 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 20% | 0.183 | 0.983 | 0.950 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 30% | 0.050 | 0.567 | 0.517 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 40% | 0.017 | 0.050 | 0.067 | 0.467 | 0.550 |

At 0 percent corruption Hebbian already fails 47 percent of the time: the stored patterns are not even fixed points past the capacity limit. Pseudoinverse and Storkey fix that but have shrinking basins; the dense memories keep full basins up to 30 percent noise. Everything collapses at 40 percent, as it must (random cues at 50 percent carry no information).

### 3. Correlated memories: p = 20, shared template, pairwise overlap rho^2

| rho | hebbian | pseudoinverse | storkey | dam-poly3 | dam-exp |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.433 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 0.2 | 0.200 | 1.000 | 0.983 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 0.4 | 0.000 | 1.000 | 0.883 | 0.850 | 1.000 |
| 0.6 | 0.000 | 1.000 | 0.283 | 0.000 | 1.000 |

Correlation is what hurts. Hebbian is dead by rho = 0.4. Polynomial memories lose their margin (a plausible reading: a rival's score grows as overlap^n, so a fixed order cannot keep up as overlap rises; not separately tested), while the exponential memory and the projection rule stay perfect. Note the pseudoinverse rule's exactness is only possible because p < N here; it breaks at alpha ~ 0.5 in experiment 1 as soon as noise is added.

### 4. Digits: ten 5x7 bitmaps (N = 35, p = 10, strongly correlated)

| rule | 10% flips, exact recall | bottom 3 rows blanked | stored digits stable |
|---|---|---|---|
| hebbian | 0.058 | 0.000 | 0.734 |
| pseudoinverse | 0.895 | 0.500 | 1.000 |
| storkey | 0.460 | 0.500 | 0.877 |
| dam-poly3 | 0.575 | 0.500 | 0.946 |
| dam-exp | 1.000 | 0.900 | 1.000 |

Blanking the bottom 3 rows is genuinely ambiguous for some digit pairs (for instance 3 and 7 both start with a full top bar), so 0.9 is not a failure of the memory alone. One recovered digit from `results/glyph_example.txt` (left: blanked cue, right: retrieved by `dam-exp`):

```
...#.   ->   ...#.
..#..   ->   ..#..
...#.   ->   ...#.
.....   ->   ....#
.....   ->   #...#
.....   ->   .###.
```

### 5. The attention connection: what beta does

Continuous states, unit-norm Gaussian patterns, d = 64, p = 32, queries = pattern + noise of norm 0.5, 5 seeds. `retrieval` counts a query as retrieved when the iterated fixed point is within 0.05 of the right pattern; `effective_patterns` is the exp-entropy of the final attention weights; `one_step_error` is the error after a single attention step.

| beta | retrieval | effective patterns | steps | one-step error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.000 | 32.0 | 6.0 | 0.978 |
| 3 | 0.000 | 31.9 | 17.1 | 0.613 |
| 6 | 0.000 | 31.3 | 43.9 | 0.098 |
| 9 | 1.000 | 1.07 | 8.4 | 0.009 |
| 12 | 1.000 | 1.01 | 5.3 | 0.001 |
| 50 | 1.000 | 1.00 | 2.0 | 0.000 |

There is a sharp transition between beta = 6 and 9. Below it the only stable fixed point is the global mean of all 32 memories (effective count ~ 32): a single attention step looks like it is homing in (error 0.098 at beta = 6) but iterating drifts away to the average. Above it each memory is a fixed point and one step is enough. With correlated memories (`rho = 0.5`, `results/beta_rho05.md`) the transition moves up to beta between 12 and 20, because the separation between patterns shrinks. This is the practical meaning of attention temperature: a head that is too soft averages values; a head that is sharp enough retrieves.

## Tests

`PYTHONPATH=src python -m pytest -q` (34 tests) covers: Storkey vs naive loop; projection idempotence; stability of stored patterns at low load and failure above 0.138 N; energy monotone per neuron update and per sweep for all three binary dynamics (and a deliberately wrong rule raising it); dense-memory sign update vs brute-force energy comparison; exponential memory storing 4 N patterns where Hebbian fails; polynomial order 5 beating order 2; continuous update equals softmax attention; continuous energy monotone for several beta; low beta gives the global mean and high beta gives exact retrieval; separation and effective-number values; the studies' qualitative claims on small instances; the CLI.

## Limits (read before quoting numbers)

* Synthetic +-1 patterns, N = 100, 3 seeds, 20 to 30 queries per seed. Error bars are not shown; treat 0.05 differences as noise.
* "Exact recovery" requires every bit correct. A softer overlap criterion would move Hebbian's cliff to a somewhat higher load.
* The exponential memory uses a fixed `beta = 12` (on overlaps normalised by N), not tuned per experiment; the stress test shows its lower tail is sensitive to this.
* No learned projections or Hopfield layers inside a trained network, no claim about large-scale capacity beyond what was run.
